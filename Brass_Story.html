FOLIO 2 / 8 · WHERE YOUR MONEY LEAKS

Five leaks, traced on the same 100 kilos.

Leak 1 · You hear the spike last.

London moves today in dollars. Your supplier calls days later in rupees. On 100 kilos, a 2% copper jump plus a slipping rupee quietly adds ~₹2,000 before you even pick up the phone.

Leak 2 · Memory does the ordering.

Buy early and cash sleeps in stock. Buy late and the jump is already in. Both mistakes repeat every few months because no week ever gets compared to a forecast.

Leak 3 · The quote rots while it waits.

A price promised to Europe three weeks ago meets today's metal. Whatever moved in between is paid by you — a few thousand rupees per container, order after order.

Leak 4 · Duties and ships ambush you.

America's 50% copper duty, on your smaller US line. Carbon taxes at Europe's and Britain's borders. Freight jumping around Africa. Each was visible weeks early to anyone watching. Nobody on your side watches.

Leak 5 · Your own bills stay silent.

Years of your purchase records know exactly when you overpay: which months, which suppliers, which sizes. That pattern is worth more than any world market data, and nobody has ever asked those files a single question.

FOLIO 5 / 8 · YOUR WEEK, IN PLAIN WORDS

Three lines. That is the whole message.

Line 1 — buy or wait.

One clear call for the week, based on where metal and the rupee are heading. Example: cover this week's production now, hold next week's quantity.

Line 2 — the safe range.

Three numbers for your rod, e.g. low ₹892, middle ₹905, high ₹918. Plan on the middle. Treat the gap to the high as your cushion.

Line 3 — the floor.

The lowest per-kilo price your export quote may carry that week, e.g. ₹940 for Europe. Quote below it and the system flags you before you send it.

No charts attached unless you ask. No new software to learn. It arrives like a message and reads like one.

FOLIO 7 / 8 · SEE THE SYSTEM

The machine you will actually touch. Drawn, not described.

Piece 1 · The whole system, in plain words. Every day the machine writes down about twenty numbers by itself: London copper and zinc closes, the rupee rate, oil and container freight, and factory signals like housing starts. Nobody in your office touches anything.

What it trains on is our full 72-dial map — 72 dials in 7 families: metal prices, the rupee, making spreads, demand signals, duties and carbon taxes, freight and fuel, and your factory's own rhythm. Every dial carries its proof in our records. Grey dials are guesses we refuse to use until proven.

How it works: your old bills teach the machine your shop's habits — how many days you trail London (usually one to two weeks), how fully a weak rupee lands in your cost, which weeks spike. Three years of world history teach it the market's habits. Every those two lessons meet and come out as three lines each buying week: buy or wait, your low-middle-high range, and the floor no export quote may cross. At week's end the machine checks what metal actually did, measures its own error, and adjusts — so a wrong week makes the next one sharper.

Piece 2 · The quote guard — and why selling sits inside a buying project. Your worst losses never happen on buying day. They happen weeks later, when metal jumps after you already promised Europe a fixed price. No buying signal can rescue an order quoted too low — so every quote is tied to the same forecast that times your buying. One brain, both hands.
