UNIT I + III1 · Observe — the people behind the price

Owner persona (Aligarh export house head). Runs a 1965-founded family unit making brass builder hardware for 40+ countries. Decides buying by feel and supplier phone calls; pain is a sudden LME spike landing mid-order. Needs a Monday-morning answer, not a lecture. Source: castlehardwares.com + LinkedIn company profile.
Purchase routine (observed pattern). Spot/mixed buying in rupees from Indian suppliers → melt/extrude in cluster → finish, pack, ship to US, Europe, Australia, UAE. No hedging, so 100% of metal + FX movement passes straight into cost. Quote validity per market decides how long a spike can be absorbed.
5W1H lens on the business. WHO decides and supplies · WHAT grades and volumes · WHEN orders and stock cover · WHERE each market's quote window · WHY margins break · HOW price, payment, scrap. The 8 owner questions in § Owner input are this lens, nothing more.

UNIT III + IV2 · Define — from vague worry to exact problem

Ill-defined → defined. “Brass is volatile and hurts us” becomes: “Castle cannot time spot purchases or set export quotes against LME copper/zinc × USD-INR plus spreads, because it has no weekly view of the three moving legs.”
5 Whys (root cause). Margins break on spikes → because buying is spot with no forward view → because no forecast exists → because drivers were never mapped → because buying runs on habit, not data. Fix the last link and the chain reverses.
Cause-effect buckets. Material (mine/smelter/scrap) · Market benchmarks (LME/COMEX) · Money (FX/rates) · Mandates (duties, CBAM, standards) · Margins (conversion, dealer, freight) · Moments (cycles, seasonality, shocks). Each bucket is a branch in §4.
Attribute listing — a forecast worth using must be: weekly, ranged (low/high, never a single number), SKU-aware (alloy weights differ), explainable (which leg moved), and cheaper than one bad purchase. Anything failing this list was discarded.

UNIT II + III3 · Ideate — options, then the shortlist

Brainstormed ideas (kept 3 of 6). ① LME-plus-premium tracker ✔ ② Weekly buy/wait signal ✔ ③ Quote & budget bands ✔ ④ Fully hedged buying ✘ (no facility) ⑤ AI black-box price picker ✘ (fails explainability) ⑥ Daily trader ✘ (factory can't act daily).
Morphological matrix. Horizons (weekly buy-timing × quarterly budgeting) · Decisions (buy / quote / budget) · Markets (US, EU, Australia, UAE). 2×3×4 = 24 cells; the system serves all of them from one forecast.
Strategic opportunities spotted. UK-India CETA live 15-Jul-2026 (99% of Indian goods duty-free into UK) · Australia ECTA at 100% from Jan-2026 · UAE CEPA corridor · EU lead-rule shift creating lead-free premium products.

UNIT IV5 · How the forces connect (no arrow without proof)

An edge appears here only if a source proves the relationship itself — plausible links were quarantined, not drawn.

FX translation → LME copper benchmark Disputed
causal · sign negative · lag contemporaneous/days · cross-level

Stronger dollar makes dollar-priced copper mechanically dearer for non-US buyers, denting demand.

Evidence: BIS WP1083 (negative assoc, strongest for Cu/tin); CME 2026-06-30 mechanical denomination. Nodes verified (FRED/ECB), beta not quantified.

Copper smelt/refine + TC/RC → Upstream physical balance Verified
causal · sign positive (tightness up -> supply down -> price up) · lag months (annual benchmark + spot lead) · same-level

Zero processing margin forces custom smelters to cut/close, capping refined output.

Evidence: Reuters Jun-2026 + MiningWeekly Nov-2025 + CRU 2026: $80+8c (2024)->$21.25+2.125c (2025)->$0/t 2026 (Jiangxi/Antofagasta); spot negative; >10% China 2026 cuts; ICSG +0.9% refined. IEA commentary corroborates.

LME copper benchmark → Secondary / scrap availability Disputed
feedback loop · sign negative feedback · lag weeks-months · cross-level

Record Cu incentivises collection + feed switch to scrap (TC-free), partly offsetting concentrate gap.

Evidence: ETC factsheet (high prices incentivise recycling); IEA (expanding recycling on high prices); ICSG secondary +5.7% vs primary +2.3%. Elasticity magnitude not evidenced.

US market access → Visible inventories Verified
causal · sign positive · lag weeks; reversible on exemption/delay · cross-level

Phased-tariff expectation widens COMEX premium, pulling metal to US and draining LME/SHFE.

Evidence: SocGen via FXStreet 10-Aug-2026 tariff-arbitrage reshapes spread; StoneX 4-way ruling; Benchmark exclusion nuance; ING/CNBC 200kt/mo US imports, COMEX record stocks at LME/SHFE expense.

Sentiment & common costs → India domestic demand level Disputed
causal · sign positive · lag 1-2 months to stocks/premia · cross-level

China ~58-60% refined use; sub-50 PMI maps to cathode/rod order cuts and SHFE builds.

Evidence: NBS PMI 50.1 Sep-2026 vs 49.8 Aug; Reuters/Mining.com copper slides as China data weighs. Event co-move only, no beta.

LME copper benchmark → Substitution drag Verified
substitution · sign negative · lag 6-18 months qualification · same-level

At >3.5-4x, cable/winding/EV qualify aluminium, shaving copper demand.

Evidence: Cu/Al 4.2-4.4 Jan-2026 (FinancialContent/FT); 3.5-4.0x threshold triggers switch (CRU/JPM); IEA Al-for-Cu saves up to 3.6Mt by 2040; Prysmian/auto cases.

LME copper benchmark → Metal value anchor Verified
causal · sign positive · lag daily · cross-level

Rs/kg = LME $/t /1000 x USD/INR x (1+duty/freight/margin). 1% INR slip ~= Rs10-13/kg at $13-14k/t.

Evidence: RBI/FBIL + ECB ref rates + CBIC tariff values; CablePriceIndia formula. Identity, no econometrics needed.

Cost of money → India domestic demand level Disputed
causal · sign negative · lag 1-3 quarters · cross-level

Higher rates curtail building/auto/grid capex to which brass demand is geared.

Evidence: JPM (Cu extremely sensitive to growth/construction); CME (rising rates curtail expansion). No Castle-specific elasticity.

Sentiment & common costs → Commercial & logistics overlay Disputed
causal · sign positive · lag 1-3 months · cross-level

$100+/bbl + Cape detour (+8-12d) + war-risk/ETS lifts Asia-Europe spot, passing to imports.

Evidence: JPM Red Sea (30% via Suez, Asia-Europe ~5x, lagged pass-through); Descartes/Hormuz surcharges; Drewry WCI route legs (URL re-verify).
