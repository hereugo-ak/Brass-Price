# Follow-up message to the founder — draft

Send as-is, or trim. Plain English, no jargon, nothing to print or file.

---

Sir, thank you for the answers — they changed the picture, and for the better.

Three things you told us matter more than the rest:

**Brass here is scrap-led.** You buy bartan and gittu scrap, and roughly 70% of raw material
comes back as scrap melted in your own shop. So the price we must track is your **brass scrap
buying price**, not some exchange number for brass — because there is no brass exchange price
to track, in India or anywhere.

**You buy weekly, in small lots.** Not in bulk, because price and availability are both
uncertain. That tells us the useful thing to give you is a **weekly range for the next month**,
not a single forecast number. A number would be false precision. A range you can plan against.

**LME leads, MCX anchors, transport is extra.** You said LME governs most of it, MCX also
governs the price in India, and transport is always on top. That is exactly the recipe we
build on. We just need your numbers to finish it.

## What we still need — four short things

**1. One recent brass purchase, costed.**
Not a bill, nothing to dig out — just from memory, what you actually paid on one recent lot:

- Brass scrap, rupees per kilo
- Transport, and how it was charged (per kilo, per tonne, per trip)
- Anything else added on top

Even approximate figures are fine. This one example tells us how far your price sits from the
metal benchmark — which is the single number that turns our range into your buying price.

**2. Transport, going forward.**
Roughly what does it cost to move a tonne of brass to your works, and from where? And when you
quote a customer abroad, is freight to the port inside your quote price or charged separately?

**3. How long a quotation stays open.**
For Europe and for the Middle East — how many days does a price you give a buyer stay valid?
Thirty days is our working assumption. If it is closer to fifteen in one market and sixty in
the other, that changes how much cushion you need in the price.

**4. A few weeks of brass prices, as they happen.**
This is the one that unlocks the model. If you or your purchase man can note, once a week, the
brass scrap price you were quoted — one line, one figure — then in two to three months we have
a real series to check our range against. Until then we are reasoning from metal prices alone,
which is good but not yet yours.

A weekly note is enough. We are not asking for records that already exist.

## What we will do with it

- Turn your buying price into a **weekly low / middle / high range for the month ahead**
- Set a **quote floor** — the lowest price you can safely commit to for a fixed-validity export
  quote, so a won order cannot turn into a loss when metal moves before you buy
- Show you plainly when the range is too wide to trust, and say so instead of guessing

We will not promise you live prices or a website. What we will do is show our working, say what
we do not know, and never put a number in front of you that we cannot trace.

Whenever it suits you, sir. Even one of the four is useful.
